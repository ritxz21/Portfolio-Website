## The problem

Large industrial companies accumulate databases the way houses accumulate
boxes in the attic. Manufacturing data here, sales data there, service
records somewhere else — each with its own schema, its own naming
conventions, and its own person who understands it.

The people who need answers are rarely the people who can write SQL. A
product manager who wants to know how a component performed across regions
last quarter has to find an analyst, explain the question, wait, and then
usually explain the question again because something got lost in
translation. The bottleneck isn't the data. It's the translation layer, and
that layer is a person.

I spent six months building two systems that attack that bottleneck from
opposite ends: one that lets people ask questions directly, and one that
makes the underlying data models legible in the first place.

## What I built: a GraphRAG query agent

{/* An architecture diagram goes here in Phase 3. */}

Instead of treating the database schema as a pile of text to search, the
system treats it as a graph. Tables are nodes, relationships are edges. When
a question comes in, the agent doesn't retrieve the most similar-sounding
table descriptions — it walks the graph from the entities in the question to
the tables that actually hold them, following joins that exist rather than
joins a language model imagined.

## What I built: a PowerBI documentation agent

The second system came out of a NASA client engagement, and it started from
a complaint rather than a spec.

PowerBI reports accumulate the same way databases do. Someone builds a
dashboard, defines forty measures, and leaves the company. The measures
still work. Nobody knows what they mean. The documentation, if it exists,
was written once and has been wrong for a year.

So I built an agent that reads the data model directly and generates
structured documentation from it — what each measure computes, what it
depends on, how the tables relate — and then lets people query that
documentation in natural language. Asking what *utilisation rate* actually
measures gets an answer grounded in the model itself, rather than in a wiki
page someone forgot to update.
